# The desert: weather, heat, sand

## Weather (`scripts/components/wind.gd`, `scripts/vfx/sand_drift.gd`)

`Wind` (node in game.tscn's root, beside `DayNightCycle`) is the ONE source of weather: `direction` (unit, always horizontal), `strength` (m/s) and `gust` (0-1), registered as `Globals.wind` the way `CameraFollow`/`RoadNetwork` register themselves — anything that should move with the weather reads it instead of inventing its own. Both the gust envelope and the direction wander are sums of sines on INCOMMENSURATE periods (27/15/57s and 90/37s, phase-offset so they don't all start the scene at zero), the trick `Flame` uses for firelight: nothing repeats on a beat you can hear. The envelope uses the WHOLE swell remapped to 0-1, not just its crest — clamping the trough away left the air dead flat half the time, which reads as calm rather than breezy — and `gust_bias` (>1) leans the middle back down so peaks still land as gusts. Defaults blow 1.5 m/s in the lull to ~6 at a peak, a strong swell every ~30s. `advance(delta)` is split out of `_process` so a probe can step the weather without waiting on real seconds.

`SandDrift` (`scenes/vfx/sand_drift.tscn`, `scripts/vfx/sand_drift.gd`) is the only sand VFX still on disk, and it is NOT instanced anywhere — blowing sand has been attempted twice and abandoned twice, so read this before a third go. PARTICLES cannot do it: a field of INDEPENDENT quads never forms a coherent bank, and every shape fails its own way — round puffs spun to a random angle and damped to a float are SNOW, thin high-contrast streaks are RAIN however they are pointed, and broad soft smears read as rectangular BLOTCHES over tarmac. VOLUMETRIC FOG (`shader_type fog` on a `FogVolume`, needs `volumetric_fog_enabled`, which has never been on despite the environment's fog settings being authored for sand) gets the softness but not the shape: Godot renders it into a coarse froxel grid with temporal reprojection (0.9 by default, which alone smears all motion away — measured 0.001 vs 0.011 screen change at 0.4), so it is SOFT BY CONSTRUCTION and gives long drifting bands but never sparse crisp waves. It also cut off at a hard horizontal line that was never explained; the line vanished with the volume, so it belonged to the fog. Two things learned that would apply to any third attempt: sampling noise isotropically off `WORLD_POSITION.xz` guarantees round blobs, so waves need the noise stretched along the wind and narrow across it, then thresholded for clear air between them; and the sand must get BRIGHTER and MORE SATURATED as it thickens (a ramp darkening to brown reads as smog, one paling to white as grey mist), with `light_volumetric_fog_energy` on the sun doing the lighting.

## Heat (`scripts/world/heat_wave.gd`, `scripts/components/player/player_heat.gd`, `shaders/heat_haze.gdshader`)

The GDD's scorch window: between 10:00 and 16:00 the sun burns whoever stands EXPOSED on foot — the health damage `PlayerEnergy` deliberately never deals. `HeatWave` (node in game.tscn beside `Wind`, `process_mode = ALWAYS` so the F9 clock scrub moves the shimmer with the sun) is the one clock authority, registered as `Globals.heat`: `is_scorching()` is the SHARP window the damage keys on, `intensity()` adds half-hour shoulders OUTSIDE it so the visual warning leads the burn in and out. The visual is the HEAT HAZE — the death grayscale's screen-reading-rect pattern, but in its own `HazeLayer` CanvasLayer at layer 0: above the world, below the HUD (layer 1), `mouse_filter` IGNORE so prompts stay clickable. `heat_haze.gdshader` displaces SCREEN_UV with two incommensurate sine fields per axis (the Wind/Flame no-beat trick), the vertical term dominant because hot air rises; a still can't show shimmer, so `heat_shot` (windowed, world/) measures FRAME-TO-FRAME motion — noon ~0.009 vs morning ~0.0009 mean RGB delta. `PlayerHeat` (component on player.tscn) ticks a 0.25s sky ray STRAIGHT UP (`COVER_MASK` walls|car|ground — a roof, a terrace shelf or a parked car shade by EXISTING, which is what will make shade-structure upgrades free; inside the window the sun rides too high for shade angles to matter at this scale) and burns `burn_per_second` (1.0 — player max health is 15, so ~15s of open noon sun kills) in whole points off a fractional accumulator, through plain `take_damage` with no attacker. A seated driver never ticks at all: `enter_vehicle` disables the whole player branch. `Signals.heat_burning_changed` drives the HUD's "SCORCHING SUN" label (`HeatInfo`, above the EN bar, the panels' shared fade pattern). THE BURN HAS TO RAISE ITS OWN FEEDBACK: damage that arrives through a `HurtBox` gets the number, the flash, a spark and blood from `HurtBox.hit`, but the sun is nobody's hit box and `PlayerHeat` calls `take_damage` directly — so it went in SILENTLY. `scorch()` now borrows the two that fit (`spawn_damage_label` + `flash`, no spark or blood: the sky is not hitting anything) and the `HeatSmoke` emitter on player.tscn cooks off the body while `burning`, world-space so it trails a walker. Authoring traps this cost: a hand-written `Curve` `_data` block bakes to ZERO and silently collapses particle scale (drop the scale curve and use `scale_min/max`), and wisps around a 60px character are easy to miss in a still — three captures were called "no smoke" while it was rendering the whole time, so `heat_shot` TOGGLES the emitter and measures the frame delta (~0.02 vs a ~0.001 floor) instead of trusting an eyeball. The shot run also exposed that the HP BAR OPENED 0/max on a fresh scene — `health_updated` had no first emit (`PlayerEnergy` defers `push_state` for exactly this) — so `Character.apply_data` pushes it now. Probe: `heat_probe` (16 checks: boundaries and shoulders, the burn, roof shade, off-hours, wiring, the real-scene burn + label).

COLD FUEL is canon (`Docs/Design/Fuel.md`): the fuel is a coolant, which is why the land drinks it in place of water and why `FuelBarrel` (`scenes/props/fuel_barrel.tscn`, `scripts/world/fuel_barrel.gd`, kit-extracted Resource Bits barrel) holds the scorch off the home base — the user's thematic answer to a heat-safe station. A fueled barrel during the scorch window VENTS a 7m cool pocket: its `CoolArea` (the shared component — see Conventions) registers into `PlayerHeat.pockets` (broadphase-maintained; `sheltered()` re-checks `venting()` LIVE — the component delegates it to the barrel, because a barrel can run dry underfoot), it drains `drain_per_hour` (2L per scorch game-hour; `advance()` split from `_process` for probe-stepping), and refills from the carried jerry can (HOLD, the pump-nozzle pattern; auto-plumbing from pump stock is the first authored Gas Stop upgrades entry, future). Fill level saves. THE DRIP IS THE SEAL, NOT THE VENT: a fueled barrel weeps black stains (the blood-pool machinery in a fuel colorway) round the clock through `leak()` — it originally dripped only while venting, and the stains all but vanished from view once the pump inherited the station pocket (`ca0c42f` removed the stationed barrels) and the survivor only vented 10:00-16:00; the decoupling is the fix, decided 2026-08-12. Only the drain stays scorch-gated. The screen haze STILLS to `POCKET_HAZE` while the player stands in a pocket — `HeatWave` lerps `haze_level`, which is why `heat_shot` lets the level CONVERGE (90 frames) before each capture pair or the drain-out pollutes the motion baseline. THE STATION'S OWN POCKET IS THE PUMP'S: gas_pump.tscn carries a 15m `CoolArea` and `GasPump.venting()` answers "stocked" — no drain of its own, selling the fuel IS the drain — wide enough for the garden, locker and bedroll, which is what makes the barrel the PORTABLE pocket rather than the base's only one (game.tscn still stands one at HomeBase). The pump joins `chill_source` too, so the forecourt damps like a barrel's ring, its strength fading over the last `DRY_SHARE` (10%) of the tank: a good day's selling visibly dries the ground out from under the garden. `barrel_probe` (21 checks: barrel mechanics in the gym — including the cool-morning leak and the dry barrel's held drip timer — the pump's stocked/dry pocket and its coverage, and both sources painting the chill array).

BARRELS ARE CARRIED, NOT PACKED (2026-08-12): too big for any inventory, a standing barrel is picked up whole (`PlayerCarry`, component on player.tscn) and set down through the build ghost's exact grammar on the same `BuildGrid` cells — AT ARM'S REACH (`PLACE_RANGE` 3m, re-checked inside `place_down` itself rather than trusted to the tint, so no input path can lay a barrel across the map) — see Station.md for the grid's adoption of authored barrels that makes this save-coherent, the ghost sanitization that keeps a preview from advertising "Pick up" out of the player's own arms, and the marked-ground refusal that keeps it off the crops. THE COLD MOVES SLOWLY IN BOTH DIRECTIONS, and one number carries all of it: `FuelBarrel.warmth` scales `chill_strength()`, ramping 0→1 over ~5s on any standing barrel (`WARM_RATE`) and back down after a pickup — so the sand tint AND the grass, which both read `chill_strength`, arrive and leave slowly for free. A picked-up barrel is NOT freed: `begin_fade()` leaves the node as its own lingering cold — mesh hidden, collision zeroed, offer retracted, seal dry, `venting()` hard false (no coolant effect on the move, the card's rule) — while `warmth` runs down and the node frees ITSELF at zero. `FADE_RATE` (0.2) is deliberately BELOW `GrassField.growth_rate` (0.25): the tufts chase the falling target all the way down, so the free lands on zero growth instead of popping the last third of a patch. That free also exposed a latent trap: `GrassField.chill_of`'s typed `Node` parameter REJECTS a freed source at the call boundary — script error, not a false — before any `is_instance_valid` inside could answer; the growth loop now validity-checks at the call site (demolition always had this window, one scan-second wide; the fade made it routine). THE BARREL'S ONE POINT PICKS ITS JOB BY THE HAND, the crop spot's rule: a fuelled can in the ACTIVE slot offers the pour (`held_can`, in-hand — though `refill()` still falls back to any carried can, so probes and future plumbing pour unposed), anything else offers "Pick up". The dispatch (`use_point`) tells a fresh press from a rolling hold by FRAME GAP, not by `is_action_just_pressed`: hold calls land on consecutive frames, and a clicked prompt injects no input at all (`try_to_interact` calls the callback directly) — so a held pour that drains its can mid-stream can never roll over into hoisting the barrel on the next frame, while a click still picks up. WHILE CARRIED: quick bar hidden (`Signals.carry_state_changed`), every draw/equip refused at the ONE gate all UI paths funnel through (`PlayerInventory.select_slot`/`equip` overrides), no drinking, no build mode, no firing (`holds_attack`, the shared claim chain) — the barrel IS the loadout. The arms borrow `gen/running_holding_rifle` as an upper_state pose (input_15; a clip in the library alone animates NOTHING — the throw's lesson, third time paid) over an authored `CarriedBarrel` mesh on player.tscn — and because that clip is a RUN cycle, `drive_pose` slews the upper layer's own time scale (`set_upper_speed`, the bolt-work's knob — free here because no weapon can be drawn mid-carry) between 0 on still feet and 1 walking, so the pose HOLDS instead of jogging the arms in place; `end_carry` restores 1.0, or the next reload's stat-scaled clip would inherit the freeze. Involuntary drops go through `force_drop` (ring-search outward from underfoot, since the player's own body blocks the centre cell): entering a car, and `Bedroll.sleep` BEFORE saving — a carried barrel is nowhere the save can see. Death just ends the carry; the reload restores the world anyway. `carry_probe` (world/, 26 checks: adoption, both offer contexts, the empty-handed carry, the slow fade and the seamless free, grid placement with the fuel riding, force_drop, the bedroll drop).

GRASS GROWS WHERE THE GROUND IS DAMP: `GrassField` (`scripts/world/grass_field.gd`, instanced into `World/Map`) reads the SAME `chill_source` group the sand tint does, so the pocket that darkens the ground is the pocket that grows something — one patch per source, sown once from a seed made of the source's own position (a patch that reshuffled as it thickened would crawl), rayed down onto the terrain so nothing floats or grows over the void edge, and rebuilt if a source ever moves. IT ROOTS IN LISTED GROUND ONLY (`surfaces`, sand): the tarmac is its own body tagged `cement` and laid proud of the sand, so the same ray that finds the ground refuses the road — and anything a ray lands on TOP of (a crate, a building, the barrel) is untagged, which is also a no. A rejected spot is simply grass that never grew, so a pocket straddling the road grows less for it rather than crowding what sand is left. IT ALSO NEEDS ROOM (`clearance`, 0.8m), because a tuft BILLBOARDS: one sown at a prop's foot passes bare ground under a ray and then swings its quad through the pump as the camera turns. The test is a ball resting ON the ground against the walls layer alone — the floor and every terrain piece carry walls|ground, so a ball that dipped would find the ground itself everywhere. Props with NO collider to be found by (a `CropSpot` is a bare Marker3D, which is why grass grew straight through the plots) join the `no_grass` group and push the same clearance. CLEARANCE IS A FADE TARGET, NOT A FACT OF THE LAND (reworked 2026-08-13): a candidate spot that fails clearance is kept as a DEAD tuft rather than dropped, every tuft carries its own `alive` flag and `grown` value, and the per-frame easing moves each one toward `chill * alive` alone — so `Signals.structure_changed` (emitted by `BuildGrid` on place and demolish, and by a barrel's `begin_fade`) now only RE-JUDGES the flags (`refresh_around` → `refresh_alive`) and the layout NEVER re-rolls: a new building fades the grass under it out over seconds, pulling it down grows the exact same tufts back, and a carried barrel's pickup-and-place no longer snapped every overlapping patch's layout in one frame, which read as the whole field updating instantly. Two traps this rework surfaced: `sow` must draw ALL of a candidate's randomness up front (position AND scale) so the RNG sequence never depends on what stands nearby, and `rebuild` had been scaling every instance by the bare `scale` — the NODE's own, always 1 — instead of the computed `current_scale`, so the growth easing and the rim taper had never actually rendered; the instance scale is what the shader reads as growth. (`alive`/`grown` are plain Arrays, NOT PackedArrays, deliberately: a packed array held in a Dictionary is copy-on-write and `patch.grown[i] = x` would mutate a copy.) THE VARIANT IS THE TUFT'S OWN, fixed at sow: `rebuild` used to deal tufts into the variant multimeshes round-robin BY DRAW ORDER, so one blade crossing the 0.01 cull re-dealt every tuft after it into a different mesh — a field-wide reshuffle from a single fade, which is exactly what a slow per-tuft fade produces constantly; order within one multimesh is invisible (a bucket is just a set of transforms), so bucket-stability is the whole fix. Ground that can never take grass (tarmac, the void) is still dropped at sow — permanent verdicts stay out of the arrays. The refresh WAITS A FULL FRAME (`pending` stamps `Engine.get_process_frames()`): a demolished building keeps its collider until the free lands at the end of the frame, and draining early fences off the ground it just left — which cost exactly one tuft in the probe before the stamp went in. A source genuinely MOVED >0.5m still re-sows fresh (different seed, different layout, `grown` starts over) — gameplay never moves a source any more; only probes drag them. The scatter CROWDS THE MIDDLE (`falloff` as the exponent on the sampled reach; 0.5 would spread it evenly) and tufts taper to `RIM_SCALE` at the edge, because the wet is deepest at the source and a ring of even grass reads as a crop circle. Each tuft's OWN `grown` eases toward `chill_strength()` while it stands clear (see the clearance rework below), so a full barrel thickens its patch in over seconds and a dry one takes its grass with it — the same warning as the ground drying out, in green. The tufts are seven single-sided KayKit forest planes (`_tools/asset_extractor/extract_grass.gd` → `assets/meshes/environment/props/grass/*_color_5.tres`), which is why they need their own `grass_material.tres` rather than the kit's shared one — billboarding `nature_material_a` would turn every tree and rock in the kit to face the camera too. THAT MATERIAL IS AUTHORED, NOT EXTRACTED (the tool only hangs it on the meshes; one that rebuilt it would flatten the art on the next run) and runs `shaders/grass.gdshader`, which does the Y-billboard ITSELF: a `StandardMaterial3D` gives it for free, but the sway needs a vertex shader, and a custom vertex shader has to write `MODELVIEW_MATRIX` (plus the scale block, because the instance scale IS the growth, and `MODELVIEW_NORMAL_MATRIX`, or the lighting goes with it). THE SWAY RIDES `wind_path`, the same accumulated metres of air travel the sand ripples ride and which `GrassField.blow` counts off `Globals.wind` exactly as `SandField` does — NEVER `TIME`, which a gust would jump the phase of. The crest marches downwind (`sway_length` metres apart), the bend is squared up the blade so the root holds, and it leans along the billboard's screen-right, so wind blowing ACROSS the view bends them most and wind blowing at the camera barely does. `grass_shot` pins the rule: with the air dead, thirty frames of `TIME` must not move a pixel. The tufts are DRY, not green: authored as forest-atlas `Color5`, those UVs land on the nature atlas's orange column, and scrub the colour of the sand it stands in is the look — a cool pocket grows something, not a lawn. `PALETTE_SHIFT` in the extractor repaints all seven at once if that ever changes (8 columns of 0.125; -0.5 is the greens). They render through MultiMeshInstance3D children authored one per variant (shadows off — a billboard's shadow gives the trick away), so a patch is a mix rather than a field of clones. DENSITY IS TWO KNOBS and the big pocket hits the second one: `density` is tufts per m2 of pocket, `max_tufts` the per-pocket ceiling — the pump's 15m pocket asks for 318 at 0.45 and is cut to 300, so raising density alone will not thicken the forecourt. Probes: `grass_probe` (19: pockets claimed, nothing outside the radius, nothing floating, thicker in the middle than the ring, nothing up against the pump or through a plot, a building clearing its own grass and giving it back when demolished, nothing rooted in anything but sand once the barrel is parked on the road, the climb and the die-back) and `grass_shot` (windowed, 4: the silhouette from a quarter turn round matching the one from the front, which is what proves the billboard, and the sway moving on the air's travel alone).

THE FLAT IS NOT FLAT: two scales sit under everything else and neither takes wind or time, because this is the GROUND and not the weather. GRAIN is the sand's tooth (`grain_size` 12cm), and it carries a SLOPE as well as a dapple — a colour speckle alone reads as dirt on a pane of glass; it is the sun catching the relief that reads as sand. The slope comes from `speck_at`, which returns value and gradient from ONE lattice fetch: three `value_noise` taps would spend 12 hashes a pixel over the whole screen estimating what the quintic already knows exactly. TWO lattices, turned to different angles — the first version used one, and the exact gradient printed the value grid across the desert as a quilt of squares, which is the same trap `turned_noise` exists to dodge. It fades out where a pixel covers more than a speck (`fwidth`), or the far sand boils. TONE is the slow sweep above it (`tone_size` 18m, a tenth of a stop), so a hundred metres of flat is not one poured colour; both multiply the FINISHED colour, so ruts, damp and snakes keep their own. `sand_shot` measures the cost as well as the look, and measures it LAST and ALTERNATED: a capture reads the framebuffer back and stalls the frames after it, which is enough on its own to bill whichever setting was measured first — that artefact alone charged the grain 1.13ms it does not cost. The claim is against the instrument's own spread rather than a hand-picked bar, and on this machine the tooth disappears into it.

THE GROUND SAYS WHERE THE POCKETS ARE: `chill_spots` on the sand shader — fed by `SandField.push_chill` from the `chill_source` group (barrels AND the pump; anything answering `chill_radius`/`chill_strength` joins), one vec4 per spot (x,z position, y strength, w radius; strength fades with the last liters, so the ground drying out is the warning), packed live-entries-first and zero-padded, which `barrel_probe` asserts as an invariant rather than pinning slot order — damp-darkens a WET-SAND patch under each source and thins the streamers crossing it (damp sand pins the snakes). The look is three things, none of them a sheen: the tint is TWO colours (`chill_color` margin → `chill_deep` core) and both are multiplied in, so what matters is which channel is darkened MOST — taking BLUE down deepens the sand's own orange the way water does, while the intuitive "cool it down" (red down) only greys it into ash; the rim WANDERS and the inside is BLOTCHY off two shared noise fields (`chill_edge` as a share of the radius, `chill_patch` metres per blotch, `chill_mottle`), pushing the sampled DISTANCE around rather than the threshold so blotches stay put as a pocket grows; and the ripple relief dies off (`chill_calm`) because moisture pins the grains. NEVER GIVE THIS GROUND SPECULAR. A wet sheen was tried and shipped a bug: Godot builds `VIEW` from the VIEW-SPACE POSITION, so under an ORTHOGRAPHIC camera it still radiates from a point instead of being constant, which parks the specular hotspot at a fixed offset from the CAMERA — and `CameraFollow` tracks the player, so a glossy patch grows a BRIGHT SPOT THAT WALKS WITH HIM, visible only on damp ground because dry sand has `SPECULAR = 0`. The whole-desert gloss bisect had already shown it (one white glint, not a sheen) and it was misread as a ripple normal coincidence; the user found it in play. `ROUGHNESS`/`SPECULAR` are constants, and the wet read is colour and blotching alone. `chill_at` early-outs on a cheap radius test before touching the noise, since almost none of a 120m floor is near a pocket. NOTE the visual rim wanders but the SHELTER is still the CoolArea's hard sphere, so damp ground and safe ground are no longer exactly the same outline — `chill_edge` is the dial if that ever matters. Send the array as PackedVector4Array: a PackedColorArray gets colour-converted in transit and world coordinates come out mangled. `barrel_shot` (windowed) proves the tint by toggling the spots and diffing the WHOLE frame (~0.045 vs a ~0.002 creep floor) and saves `user://barrel_pocket.png` for the eyeball pass — screen-space sample placement was tried five ways and lost every time, because `CameraFollow.apply_zoom_anchor` offsets the camera node per-frame off the live cursor, so `unproject_position` does not land where things draw. When a shader effect "doesn't show", bisect with unconditional shader paints (hardcoded disc, raw-uniform-as-ALBEDO) before touching the data path — two of tonight's three "fixes" broke a working system chasing a misread capture (SDFGI occlusion blobs pass convincingly for a radial tint at these radii).

SAND TRACKS (`shaders/sand_field.gdshader` — renamed from sand_tracks once it grew ripples, streamers and chill spots; it is the whole desert surface now, paired with the `SandField` wiring script — prototyped in `_tools/probes/tracks/`) are the OTHER direction and this one works, IN `game.tscn`: a SubViewport looking straight down renders trail particles (render layer 2, `local_coords` off) into a texture the ground shader reads as a HEIGHT FIELD — real displacement plus normals recomputed from the gradient, not a decal. Coverage went FIXED: a 2048² buffer over the whole 120x120 map = 5.9cm/texel, the exact density the look was tuned at, nothing forgotten. The wiring is `SandField` (`scripts/world/sand_field.gd`, node in `World` and in the probe scene — one per scene with a displaced-sand floor): hands the buffer to the material, frames the trail camera on the ground (`trail_origin` uniform, so the shader maps world space, not object space), and feeds the live wind + accumulates `wind_path`; `follow_wind` off holds authored wind values for tuning. The stamps are `SandTrail` (`scripts/components/vehicle/sand_trail.gd`, node in `vehicle_base.tscn` — every vehicle lays tracks): one `scenes/vfx/sand_trail_stamp.tscn` per wheel, mounted on the BODY at the wheel's footprint — NEVER on the VehicleWheel3D, which spins with the tyre and orbits a child stamp into a beaded dotted track — gated per frame on speed AND `SurfaceData.displaced_tracks` (sand true, tarmac false, so roads never collect invisible ruts). The main camera's `cull_mask` excludes render layer 2 or it draws the raw white stamps over the world. Four things it cost to learn: the trail buffer SATURATES — enough stamps overlap that every rut reads 1.0 — so how obvious a track looks cannot be tuned by driving and needs the `rut_strength` uniform, which scales the sampled height so tint, trench and shading dim together; `GPUParticles3D` defaults to `fixed_fps = 30`, which at 12 m/s spaces the stamps 40cm apart and beads the track into a dotted line (set 0); tracks FADE via an alpha ramp on the process material, which needs `vertex_color_use_as_albedo` on the dot or the particle colour never reaches the mesh, and the ramp must be pre-compensated for the saturation (down to 0.35 by 30% of life) or the mark holds full strength then collapses at the end; and the buffer CANNOT be measured with `get_image()` — that un-premultiplies a transparent-background viewport, so every empty texel reads bright — measure the rendered frame instead. Persistence IS the particle lifetime. Sand therefore sets `skid_marks = false` (see SurfaceData), so the two systems never draw the same slide. FEET WRITE THE SAME BUFFER (2026-08-12, spawn path settled 2026-08-13): `CharacterFootsteps` — which already knows the exact plant frame per foot bone — stamps each plant through `stamp_at(foot spot)`, gated on `SurfaceData.at` sampled AT THE SPOT rather than under the body (a foot can hang over the tarmac's edge while the chest stands on sand) and on `displaced_tracks`, the wheels' exact rule, so roads take no prints; the mark's HEIGHT is the spot's own too, never the body's — a probe once parked the body off the map edge and every print spawned down the void with it. EACH PRINT IS ITS OWN ONE-SHOT BURST (`scenes/vfx/footprint_stamp.tscn`: the wheel dot at a 0.32 quad, `amount` 6, `one_shot`, `explosiveness` 1, freed on `finished`): `emit_particle` on a never-emitting system is SILENTLY DISCARDED — it counted prints while rendering none — and pulsing a shared emitter's `emitting` has restart semantics of its own; a fresh one-shot is the spawn path with no ambiguity. Every character prints, enemies included, since the component is character_base's. THE INSTRUMENT COST MORE THAN THE FEATURE: `steps_shot`'s first cut PASSED at 0.023 on pure drift (the stamp scene shipped a hallucinated `visible = false` the wheel recipe never had), and it took holding the WHOLE observation still to expose it — the sand's `wind_path` frozen, the GRASS field's own accumulator frozen, the eased camera parked on a dead anchor then disabled (it never truly stops, and a sub-pixel pan touches every edge pixel), the body walked out of frame ON THE MAP, the HUD's rangefinder and perf text excluded by `frame_diff`'s centre band — after which the floor read 0.0000 and the amplified pixel-diff finally showed the fourteen stamped dents and nothing else. The claim is now a HOT-SAMPLE COUNT, not a frame mean: point features water a mean down into exactly the noise it took three broken instruments to stop measuring; prints give ~17 hot samples on a still frame, drift and nothing give none. `footprint_probe` (characters/, 6, counts `prints_stamped` and the live burst) and `steps_shot` (windowed, world/, the pixels).

The same shader carries the WIND-OVER-SAND look, and the split matters (a dense ripple field visibly crawling reads as grain flowing down a dune, not wind — measured against the aeolian literature): the ripple BED is quasi-static (real ripples migrate over minutes; `ripple_drift` is a small share of wind travel) and the wind read comes from STREAMERS — "sand snakes", sparse pale filaments racing along the wind at `streamer_speed` (~0.4, physics says 1/3-1/2 of wind speed), flooding in with a gust and draining in the lull (`wind_gust` moves the threshold: MORE snakes, not wider). Construction lessons: snakes are RIDGE noise (its peaks are connected forking filaments; plain thresholded noise gives smeared patches) in wind-stretched coordinates (~7:1), domain-warped first or filaments jog in right angles along the lattice; the LANES and the SAND move at decoupled speeds (`streamer_march` is the pattern's share of streamer speed, the pulse field races at full speed THROUGH it) — one shared speed slides the whole pattern as a printed sheet, the exact complaint a moving texture earns; and the ORIENTATION is a knob outcome, not a mode — `streamer_length`/`streamer_gap` are the stretch along/across the wind, so swapping their magnitudes (e.g. 1.5/10 instead of 9/1.2) turns the snakes into cross-wind WAVE FRONTS, which want a high `streamer_march` (~0.6) so the bands visibly sweep downwind; noise lattices must each be TURNED (`turned_noise`) or the shared grid prints as rectangles; one octave at patch scale reads as blocks (too few cells in view — use `fractal_noise`); quintic interpolation, not smoothstep, for anything feeding a NORMAL (smoothstep's second derivative prints cell edges). ALL motion keys off `wind_path` — the air's accumulated travel in metres, integrated on the CPU each frame — never TIME: TIME times a varying wind strength jumps every phase on every gust. The probe's `follow_wind` export feeds the live `Globals.wind` (direction/strength/gust); off holds the authored material values steady for tuning, while still advancing `wind_path` so the ground keeps moving.
