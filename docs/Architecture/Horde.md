# Horde

## Horde (`scripts/world/horde.gd`, `scenes/world/horde.tscn`)

Zombies are not characters. A `Horde` node owns every zombie as packed arrays (position, yaw, speed, health, state, clip, phase, variant, timers) with no node and no physics body per zombie, and steps them all in one `_physics_process` loop: a spatial hash on 2 m cells gives separation and neighbour queries, and the whole thing renders through one `MultiMeshInstance3D` per outfit variant. Measured on 2026-09-22: 500 zombies tick in about 2.3 ms average, 3.3 ms worst, in GDScript, drawn in 16 draw calls. Hundreds on screen is the design target; thousands would want the loops in C# or GDExtension, not a different design.

BEHAVIOUR IS DUMB ON PURPOSE. `hunt_target()` is the player's vehicle when they are seated, else the player, or an explicit `target_node` (probes set it). Beyond `AGGRO_RANGE` a zombie idles; inside it, it seeks the target plus separation, yaw-lerped at `TURN_RATE`, at its own walk or run speed (`runner_share` of them run). Within `REACH_VEHICLE` of a vehicle's centre (or `REACH_CHARACTER` of a body) it attacks: the attack clip loops and lands `bite_damage` once per loop at `ATTACK_HIT_SHARE` of the clip, through the target's `hurt_box.hit()` so the truck's damage feedback and `VehicleDamage` fire as for any hit; the `AttackData.attacker` is the Horde node. No navmesh, no vision, no cover: on the flat desert seek plus separation is enough, and props are walked through for now.

RAMMING AND SHOOTING GO THROUGH THE HORDE, NOT PHYSICS. `rammed()` tests each zombie against the target vehicle's local hull box (`hull_half_extents`) whenever the vehicle moves faster than `RAM_MIN_SPEED`, and a hit becomes a FLUNG state: a scripted arc (`RAM_LIFT`, a share of the vehicle's velocity) that dies on landing. `intersect_ray(from, to)` is the hitscan's horde test — zombies are vertical capsules (`BODY_RADIUS`, `BODY_HEIGHT`) against the flat shot line, nearest along the ray wins — and `WeaponRanged.fire` calls the static `Horde.hit_test` beside its hurt-box and surface rays, so the nearest of the three takes the tracer; a horde hit schedules `damage()` and a `FleshImpact` with the tracer's delay, and `damage()` flashes the instance (custom data `y`, the shader tints it), shoves it along the shot, and kills at zero health. Kills emit `zombie_killed(position)`; the corpse plays the death clip, holds its last frame for `CORPSE_TIME`, then frees the slot. A slot's `generation` counter lets a `HordeTarget` (a RefCounted with `global_position`/`is_dead`, what `CrewGunner` holds and `EnemyWeapons.fire_from` reads by duck typing) notice its zombie died and the slot was reused.

RENDERING: only zombies within `VIEW_RADIUS` of the camera focus (`Globals.camera_follow`, else the target) are written into the multimesh buffers each tick; `visible_instance_count` does the rest and a huge `custom_aabb` stops the multimesh being culled while its instances move. Each instance's custom data carries the animation FRAME (x) and the hit flash (y). `shaders/horde_skin.gdshader` skins in the vertex shader from a bone-matrix texture: `HordeAnimationSet` (`assets/animations/zombie/horde_animation_set.res`, baked by `_tools/asset_extractor/bake_horde_animations.tscn`, a scene run because it needs the base's autoloads) holds, per frame and per bone, the 3x4 skinning matrix `global_pose * inverse_bind` as three RGBAF texels, and `texture()` uploads it; the shader reads `BONE_INDICES`/`BONE_WEIGHTS` from the mesh and blends four bone matrices. One bake serves every outfit because all parts share the rig. The bake is SAVED WITH `ResourceSaver` DIRECTLY: `ExtractLib.save_keeping_uid` rewrites text and truncated the binary `.res` to four bytes the first time. Clips in the set are listed in the baker's `CLIPS`; the kit's blend had `blender/animation/limit_playback` on by default, which clipped every action to the scene's 43-frame playback range until it was switched off in both `.import` files.

VARIANT MESHES: `extract_outfits.gd` reads the kit's 60 preset characters into `data/outfits/presets/preset_NNN.tres`, and `extract_horde_meshes.gd` merges each outfit's parts into ONE ArrayMesh under `assets/meshes/horde/` (one material, bone indices remapped from each part's skin bind order to the rig's bone order); 120 of the 245 parts carry EIGHT weights per vertex, so the merge keeps the four strongest and renormalises. Sixteen variants are merged by default (`VARIANTS`), about 11k triangles each.

Probes: `world/horde_probe` (500 zombies: spawn, approach, tick budget, draw count, bite, hitscan kill, ram) and the windowed `world/horde_shot`. The first probe run had the bare `vehicle_base` falling through the floor for lack of a collision shape, which read as the horde fleeing at 40 m/s.
