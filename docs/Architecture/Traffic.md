# Traffic and the delivery truck

THE DELIVERY TRUCK DRIVES ITSELF (`scripts/world/delivery_service.gd`, `scripts/components/vehicle/delivery_ai.gd`). `DeliveryService` (node in `World`, exported `bay` → the `DeliveryArea` Area3D) listens to `Signals.calendar_updated` and sends one truck in at `arrive_hour` (08:00), from a random road entry point; the DATE it last served is the guard, since the signal fires every game minute. `DeliveryAI` mirrors `VehicleAI` exactly — `StateMachine` over `VehicleSteering` — with arrive → park → depart. Arrival is decided by the BAY'S OWN SENSOR (`at_bay()`: the Area3D's overlapping bodies, mask 8 = car layer, within `BAY_RANGE` 3.5m), not by reaching the centre: a truck's turning circle is far wider than `VehicleSteering.GOAL_DISTANCE`, so insisting on the exact spot just orbits it — the same reason `GasPump.can_serve` asks its `CarArea`. The bay stands off the tarmac, so `drive_to(..., p_exact = true)` appends the destination itself after the route's last road point — THREADED through `approach_points()`, a lead-in off the bay's +Z the road leg routes to, so the last leg comes in straight along an authored line. THE REAL PUMP CLEARANCE IS THE BAY'S PLACEMENT though: the DeliveryArea stands WEST of the pump (moved from beside it), giving every turn a truck makes metres of air by construction — nudge the node, not constants, when the forecourt grows furniture; three geometry-cleverness attempts (long lead-ins the waypoint-drop ate, a rotated two-via dogleg the goal ring missed) lost to that one scene edit. Arrival RETRIES a give-up like customers do (4s wait, 3 tries — the blocker is usually a customer mid-fill, and those move) before the park-short fallback; the truck once opened shop in the driving lane 14m from its bay, which is why `traffic_jam_probe` now asserts the PARKED POSITION (< 6m from the bay), not just the state, and both it and `delivery_probe` count chassis-on-pump contact ticks per leg (the grind that motivated all of this measured 554-994 ticks before the fix; zero after, both entry forks). Park kills the engine and enables the truck's ONE `InteractiveArea` (the trade menu); `leave_hour` (15:00) closes it and drives the truck back off the map, where `errand_finished` frees it. THE HOURS ARE A SPAN, NOT TWO INSTANTS (fixed 2026-08-17): both gates ask `Calendar.past_hour`, never `hour == x`, because the clock JUMPS — a debug scrub, a sleep, a loaded save — and an exact test that misses its instant stalls the day forever. As played: scrubbing past 15:00 left the truck parked at the bay with its counter open until midnight, and the arrive gate had the same hole seven hours earlier, where a scrub past 08:00 silently cost the day its delivery. A clock that jumps the WHOLE span owes nothing but must still BANK the date, or the unserved day retries every minute until midnight. AND THE HOUR COMES BACK ROUND, which no `past_hour` can see: sleeping at the counter rolls the date with 15:00 sitting in the future again, so `begin()` stamps the `leave_date` the visit belongs to and `time_to_leave()` calls any later date overdue — the hour test alone held the truck at its bay into the next day, and the banking above then cost that day its own delivery. `delivery_probe` now scrubs to 16:00 instead of landing on 15:00 — landing exactly on the hour is what let the trap live through 14 green checks.

The counter is ONE POINT: `TradeArea` ("Trade") opens `InteractionMenu` (`scenes/ui/menus/interaction_menu.tscn`, instanced in game.tscn's Overlay; `Signals.interaction_menu_requested(title, options)` where options are `{label, target, callback}` rows) — a paused popup of InputKeyButtons picked by number key or click, closed by ESC or the scrim, action called AFTER close so one that opens another screen re-pauses cleanly. "Sell goods" opens the truck's own `Inventory` (`data/items/storage/truck_counter.tres`) through `Signals.container_opened` — anything DROPPED IN IS BOUGHT at `ItemData.price`, but only as the truck LEAVES (`settle_sale`, called from `DeliveryParkState.exit()`). Goods therefore SIT on the counter all day and a wrong item can be dragged back out — buying on contact instead read as the item vanishing, since it never stayed long enough to be seen. There is still no sell key to press and nothing can drive away unpaid, and emptying the grid at departure can't strand the open UI because every screen pauses the tree: `Calendar`'s timer is pausable, so the clock cannot reach `leave_hour` while somebody is looking at the counter. "Buy NNL ($X)" restocks the pump: `affordable_liters` buys whole liters and never a rounded cost the purse can't cover, because `GasPump.buy_stock` is all-or-nothing. The menu computes its labels AT OPEN — it pauses the tree, so nothing can move under a stale price.

A HEAVY vehicle needs `suspension_max_force` raised (default 6000 N): at the truck's 1600 kg the springs bottomed out, the chassis hull sat ON the road (`get_contact_count()` 3), and full throttle moved it 4 cm/s. Stiffness and travel made NO difference — the per-wheel force cap was the binding constraint. Anything heavier than the 800 kg car needs the same check.

## Traffic (`scripts/world/road_network.gd`, `scripts/components/vehicle/`)

Customer cars route themselves to the pump, fill up, pay and leave. The split mirrors the enemy AI exactly: `VehicleAI` (brain + `StateMachine`) over `VehicleSteering` (wheels-level driving), the way `EnemyAI` sits over `EnemyMovement`.

ROADS ARE SPLINES (`World/Roads`, a `StaticBody3D` running `RoadNetwork`): every `Path3D` child is a road, and one pass over the curves produces BOTH the traffic graph and the tarmac, so the two can never disagree. Each curve is sampled every `SAMPLE_STEP` (3m) into `AStar3D` points chained along the run, and the same samples extrude a `road_width` (7m) ribbon into the child `Surface` MeshInstance3D plus a `ConcavePolygonShape3D` on the child CollisionShape3D — layer 16, `metadata/surface = "cement"`, sitting `surface_height` (2cm) proud so rays tag the road rather than the sand. THE RIBBON WEARS `shaders/road.gdshader` (`road_material.tres`), which does two things a flat albedo could not. The asphalt tile is world-tiled GRAIN over an authored `road_color` rather than the albedo itself: the tile is nearly black, and its average in the space the shader samples is 0.015 — NOT the 0.13 the pixels look like, because `source_color` decodes the png from sRGB, and taking the eyeballed number darkened the road to a quarter of its colour. Dividing by that average turns the tile into a multiplier around 1.0, which is what makes `grain_bite` mean "how much of its contrast to take". And THE SAND EATS THE SHOULDERS: `sand_creep` (a share of the half-width, bent by noise so the line is ragged) plus drifts that cross the whole ribbon dissolve the road's ALPHA to nothing, so what shows through is the REAL ground — its ripples, its damp chill tint, the tyre tracks stamped into it — never a second guess at what colour the sand is, which is the trap a blend-to-`sand_color` uniform would have set. `UV.x` is what measures the distance across, so the shoulders stay the shoulders however the curve pinches. THE BAND IS DITHERED INTO GRAINS, not faded: a soft alpha ramp reads as fog lying over the tarmac, so the coverage is compared against a two-octave speck noise (`speck_size` 6cm) and each speck comes out all sand or all tarmac — the coverage only decides HOW MANY. That is what lets the band be wide (`sand_edge`) and still look sharp: the scatter thins out into single grains instead of dissolving. It is held back where a pixel can no longer resolve a speck (`fwidth`), because past that a stipple is just noise and the soft fade it came from is the better answer. The collider keeps the FULL width, so grass still refuses to grow on ground the sand has visually reclaimed. `road_shot` (windowed, 5) pins all three by toggling them — including "less blurry" as a MEASUREMENT rather than an opinion: it takes the pixels where the dithered and faded frames disagree (which is the transition and nothing else, so no colour threshold has to be guessed at — a first attempt at one caught 60% of the frame, the tarmac's own value included) and compares how much each jitters against its neighbours there. Grains alternate; a ramp slides. It PAUSES the tree first: nothing in this shader moves, so a customer car driving through the frame is pure noise — it swamped the grain check at a creep of 0.013 before the pause went in. JUNCTIONS are implicit: samples from DIFFERENT runs within `JOIN_RADIUS` are connected, so a spur whose ends sit on the highway welds itself on with no markers — but same-run pairs are deliberately skipped, since a radius as wide as the sampling would let a route hop over its own waypoints and cut the bend they were sampled to follow. The script is `@tool` and connects each `curve.changed`, so dragging a point in the editor re-lays the tarmac under it; `@export_tool_button("Rebuild road")` forces it. `entry_points()` (graph points with ONE neighbour) are where the network runs off the map — a spur that rejoins the road it left has no dead end and so is never mistaken for an exit. `entry_transform()` places an arriving car `ENTRY_INSET` (6m) INSIDE the end: parked on the end itself half the wheelbase hangs off the tarmac onto the map's rim, and the chassis grounds out before it can pull away. Registers itself as `Globals.road_network`.

The tiled predecessor is gone: `TerrainRoads`, `road_tiler.gd` and the painter's Road / Road Erase modes were all deleted (`assets/meshes/environment/roads/` and its extractors stay on disk as art for whenever the ribbon wants a texture).

`VehicleSteering` turns a route into pedal/steering intent. Two things it must do that a naive waypoint-chaser doesn't: (1) a car cannot turn tighter than ~6m, so at a 3m grid corner it ALWAYS swings wide of the inside waypoint — chasing that node by distance alone leaves it ORBITING the corner forever, because the point it aims at sits inside its own turning circle; `passed()` drops a waypoint once the car is beyond its plane (held to `PASS_DISTANCE` so it only drops corners genuinely rounded). (2) the bend you're already in is far too late to brake for, so `corner_limit()` scans the route ahead and arrives at the turn already slow. A give-up watchdog (`gave_up`, reverse-out then quit after `UNSTICK_ATTEMPTS`) means a car wedged on the player's parked car leaves instead of grinding forever. (3) a waypoint BEHIND the vehicle and inside `TURN_CIRCLE` (7m) cannot be steered to — chasing it was the truck's departure ORBIT, circling its first route point for 50s after reversing out of the bay — so `advance_waypoint` skips it and aims at the next point out, which gives the turn somewhere to go. `drive_to(target, p_exact, p_via)` appends the destination ITSELF after the route's last road point, for somewhere off the tarmac entirely (the delivery bay, a pump-side parking spot); `p_via` threads exact lead-in points before it AND the road leg routes to the FIRST via rather than the target — routing to the target marches the lane right past the turn-off and demands a backward hook to recover. `back_up(seconds)` drives straight back with no route at all: a vehicle that parked nose-in cannot turn round inside its own bay, and steering out of one is a 180 it has no room for — the delivery truck reverses to the lane first, then routes. **The car models face +Z** (front wheels sit there), NOT Godot's usual -Z — measured, not assumed.

`VehicleAI` runs arrive → refuel → depart (`scripts/components/vehicle/states/`). ARRIVAL RETRIES a give-up (4s wait, twice) before departing unserved: the steering's give-up cannot tell a wall from a queue, and the blocker on a forecourt is usually another customer mid-fill or the parked truck — both of which move. Only a car still stuck after the retries leaves, so nothing permanent can hold the forecourt hostage (the jam probe caught a customer giving up seconds before its path cleared and driving home unserved). "At the pump" is `GasPump.can_serve()` — the pump's own `CarArea` sensor, the same test behind the player's nozzle prompt, plus a `SERVICE_RANGE` — NOT "reach the cell centre": the pump stands flush with the kerb at a corner, and a customer insisting on the exact spot grinds its nose into it. The parking spot itself (`pump_stop()`) is `PARK_DISTANCE` (3.5m) out from the pump along the line to the nearest tarmac, driven to as an EXACT waypoint — a car pulls OFF the lane beside the pump the way it would at a real forecourt. Stopping on the centreline instead would put the far lane's cars out of `SERVICE_RANGE` once `keep_right` shifted them. Moving the pump still moves where customers park. Payment is ONE `sell_gas()` at the end of the fill, never a per-frame trickle — the pump rounds each sale to whole dollars, so a tenth of a litre at a time would round the takings to nothing.

SHOOT ONE AND IT RUNS: `Vehicle` emits `damaged(attack)` (the mirror of `Character.damaged`, so a brain riding the hull reacts without the hull knowing brains exist) and `VehicleAI.on_damaged` abandons whatever it was doing for `depart`, at `flee_cruise_speed`/`flee_corner_speed` rather than the speeds it arrived at. It leaves WITHOUT PAYING for free: `buy_fuel()` only runs when a fill runs out, so quitting the state early is already quitting without settling up. The one distinction that matters is WHO hit it — `crash_damage` names the vehicle ITSELF as the attacker while a bullet or a ram names somebody else, and only the latter counts. Without that test no customer ever reached the pump: they are placed a little above the road and the landing alone deals enough crash damage to send every one of them straight home. Repeat hits are harmless, since `change_state_to` no-ops onto itself and `fleeing` gates the speed-up.

`TrafficSpawner` (node in `World`) sends a car from a random entry point on a randomised interval while a pump in the `gas_pump` group has stock and is selling — so the service switch really does close the forecourt. F9's "Send a customer car in" skips the timer.

Measured on the splines: 4/4 customer errands complete (pump reached in ~14s, paid, gone in ~30s) and the truck's whole day runs clean, against 3/4 completions on the old grid.

CAR-TO-CAR AVOIDANCE (`VehicleSteering.traffic_check`): every AI vehicle watches a corridor ahead (12m long, lane-wide) for other VEHICLES — group `vehicle`, so the player's car counts — and caps its speed on the gap. CURATED STATIC PROPS join the sense through group `steer_around` (the gas pump; add furniture there as the forecourt grows): treated exactly like a vehicle at rest — dodged around AND stopped `BUMPER_ROOM` short, because a dodge term alone can never outvote a big heading error (the truck's 180 used to press its nose into the pump mid-turn; the stop feeds the wedge watchdog, whose reverse is what actually re-aims the turn) — EXCEPT anything within `GOAL_EXEMPT` (6m) of the route's goal, which is the destination's own furniture: a customer's whole errand is driving AT the pump, and the exemption is what lets the cap exist without stalling a single fill-up. Moving traffic is FOLLOWED at its own pace and flags `queueing`, which resets the wedge watchdog — waiting in line must not count as wedged — while anything at rest is stopped short of and STEERED AROUND (`dodge`; a blocker dead ahead defaults to a left pass, so two facing dodgers split to opposite sides). Nose-to-nose standoffs break by instance id: the senior vehicle creeps past at half corner speed, the junior holds, and the holder's own watchdog reverse after 2.5s is part of the choreography (it makes room). SPAWNS check occupancy first (`RoadNetwork.clear_entry`): a vehicle spawned onto an occupied entry lands ON TOP of the occupant — the jam probe caught a customer parked on the truck's roof, and the truck then dutifully yielded to the car on its own back — so the spawner and the delivery service take the other end or wait (the service loses nothing by waiting: `last_delivery` is only stamped on success, so the next minute retries). The spawner's TIMER also holds while `DeliveryService.truck_on_road()` — parked-and-trading doesn't hold it, and F9/probes force `spawn()` past the gate on purpose, which is exactly what `traffic_jam_probe` does: the 08:00 truck plus two forced customers, all three of which must complete (they clear in ~50s).

Known: with only two entry points the same lane carries both directions (`keep_right` handles most meetings). Headless probe runs are MOSTLY deterministic — nothing calls `randomize()`, but timer-driven draws race on frame alignment: identical delivery_probe runs reproduce one of a small set of traces (different entry rolls and departure lines measured 2026-08-08, all passing; the racing draw is unfound). Stage variety deliberately if a claim needs it, and never pin a probe to one exact trace. The truck's two old quirks are FIXED and probe-pinned: the pump-side grind (bay moved west + `steer_around` prop sense + approach lead-in; zero contact ticks on every measured path) and the departure orbit (the `TURN_CIRCLE` skip; departures now clear in under 30s). One rough edge remains by design: a departure that gives up mid-route `finish()`es and the service reclaims the truck wherever it stands — acceptable while nothing can permanently wall in the forecourt.
