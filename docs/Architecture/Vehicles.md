# Vehicles

## Vehicles (`scripts/vehicles/vehicle.gd`, inherited scenes)

`scenes/vehicles/vehicle_base.tscn` is the shared base (mirrors `character_base.tscn`): a `Vehicle` (`VehicleBody3D`) root carrying the script, physics defaults, `metadata/surface = "metal"`, driver seat/spawn markers, engine/ignition/squeal audio, exhaust + damage smoke, dust emitters, the damage `Flame`, a `NavigationObstacle3D`, and an INERT `HurtBox` (layer 0, non-monitorable — inert = INVULNERABLE hull: hurt rays can't find it and `crash_damage` checks `monitorable` before self-damaging). Base collision mask includes the car layer, so vehicles collide with each other. Variants INHERIT it and add meshes, chassis collision, wheels (discovered by TYPE in `_ready` — each variant lays out its own set; traction wheels = the drift/grip axle), and their `VehicleData`. `car.tscn` arms the HurtBox (layer 3 = both hurt layers, monitorable, chassis-shaped) and carries THREE precise interaction points, all on the interact key — ENTER on a cabin-wide box at the doors, trunk at the tail (z -2.2), repair at the nose (z +2.2, enabled only while damaged; `VehicleDamage` finds it by the `RepairInteractionArea` name). `delivery_truck.tscn` (`data/vehicles/delivery_truck.tres`: heavy, slow, low rumble) leaves the HurtBox inert and has NO door — it cannot be driven, damaged, or destroyed: weapon hurt rays pass through, so shots land on the chassis body, whose `metadata/surface = "armor"` override plays the RICOCHET kit (`data/surfaces/armor.tres`, `ricochet = true`: no bullet-hole decal — spark burst + flash and the streak visibly deflects off the panel, metal ping audio). The car keeps holes (it's damageable; `metal.tres`), and both share `impact_burst_metal.tscn` — smoke puffs + velocity-stretched sparks. It now carries `VehicleSteering` + `DeliveryAI` and runs the daily supply route (see Station economy).

DRIVING IS DECOUPLED FROM INPUT the same way `CharacterMovement` is: the physics reads only `control_throttle` / `control_steer` (+ is LEFT) / `control_brake`, and `_physics_process` fills them for a seated player (`gather_player_control` — the `car_accelerate`/`car_reverse`/`car_brake` actions, W/S/Shift on keys and RT/LT/B on a pad since the 2026-08-17 pad pass, with steering on the same `move_left`/`move_right` the feet use; `walk` was renamed `car_brake` — it was only ever polled as the brake) or PULLS them from a `VehicleSteering` component (`controller.gather_intent()`) — the same pull `CharacterMovement` makes on its subclasses, so there is no node process-order to get right. `is_controlled()` is "driver or controller"; anything else brakes to a stop. `start_engine`/`stop_engine` are split out of add/remove_driver because NPC traffic has no occupant to seat (and ignition must wait for `VehicleAI.begin()` — children are ready BEFORE their parent, so the Vehicle's `@onready` audio nodes don't exist during a component's `_ready`). `start_engine(p_crank = false)` SKIPS THE STARTER SOUND: a spawned vehicle was already driving somewhere beyond the map edge, and hearing it crank up out there breaks the fiction — only a car that visibly stood still cranks (a driver getting in, the truck leaving its bay with a `hold_for` beat so it finishes cranking before the wheels turn, a repair). The player's dashboard and camera lead stay gated on `is_player_driven()`.

DAMAGE IS A COMPONENT TOO — `VehicleDamage` (`scripts/components/vehicle/vehicle_damage.gd`, node in vehicle_base.tscn): ramming, crash delta-v (it captures its own `previous_velocity` pre-solve), the smoke/fire/sick-engine tiers, the explosion + debris, and the repair offer all live there; it registers as `vehicle.damage` and connects `body_entered` itself. The vehicle keeps `health`, `is_destroyed` and THIN `take_damage`/`repair` delegates, because the world enters through the root: hurt rays call `owner.take_damage` and the scene's RepairInteractionArea targets the root node. One trap the split must keep: `crash_damage`'s AttackData names the VEHICLE as attacker, never the component — `VehicleAI.on_damaged` reads `attacker == vehicle` to tell a self-inflicted landing from an enemy hit, and a component-attacker would send every spawning customer fleeing home again.

The car: `add_driver`/`remove_driver` handle the enter/exit handshake: player state → Drive, camera retarget, engine audio, HUD input hints (ACCELERATE/STEER/BRAKE) via `Signals.input_info_added`; the driver's hurt box goes non-monitorable (the car soaks the shots — its own HurtBox sits on layers 1|2 so BOTH sides' rays damage it), and the EXIT prompt only exists under 10 km/h. The car is a vision TARGET while driven (`get_faction()` = driver's, `is_dead` = destroyed). RAMMING (contact monitor): speed-scaled damage above 3 m/s with a 0.6s per-victim cooldown — slow hits shove, above 8 m/s the victim goes AIRBORNE; corpses keep the momentum (`CharacterMovement.become_corpse()`: physics-only until settled). CRASHES hurt the hull too (`crash_damage`, routed through the vehicle's OWN HurtBox so the damage label/flash/spark feedback fires): walls/terrain/props (hardness 1.0) and other vehicles (0.8) deal delta-v-scaled damage — pre-step `previous_velocity` vs post-solve velocity, so a wall scrape is free, a head-on eats the full span, and wheels-first landings never fire (suspension keeps the chassis off the ground); characters use SPEED at 0.15 hardness instead (the solver treats kinematic bodies as immovable for the contact frame — delta-v would read like a wall); one damage event per 0.5s pile-up. FUEL: `VehicleData.fuel_tank_size` knob (35-120L span), `current_fuel` runtime state on the vehicle's data — duplicated at `_ready` so vehicles sharing a .tres don't share tanks — starts full, burns idle→throttle rates while driven, `refuel(liters)` awaits the gas pump; a dry tank zeroes engine input (coasts; brakes/steering stay). The HUD is the PLAYER's dashboard: `vehicle_speed_updated` / `vehicle_fuel_updated` / `vehicle_health_updated` and `drive_state_changed` only fire when `is_player_driven()` (`driver == InputManager.player`), so NPC traffic can burn fuel and take damage without writing to it — same gate guards the camera retarget, drive input hints and EXIT prompt. `push_hud_state()` fills all three dials at once when the player gets in. Driving juice: engine pitch/volume ride speed (scaled by the `top_speed` export). THE GROUND CONVERSATION LIVES IN A COMPONENT — `VehicleGroundFX` (`scripts/components/vehicle/vehicle_ground_fx.gd`, node in vehicle_base.tscn beside `SandTrail`): dust banks, skid ribbons, tire squeal and the `sample_ground` surface ray all moved there out of vehicle.gd, and it REGISTERS itself on the vehicle (`vehicle.ground_fx = self`, the way `controller` does) because `SandTrail` reads the sampled surface through it (`vehicle.ground_fx.ground` — the rename that broke silently in the extraction until the probe suite caught SandTrail erroring every frame). The rear-grip drift lerp deliberately STAYED in vehicle.gd (`update_drift_grip`): it is handling physics, not effects. Parent-first processing means the component reads the same tick's `speed`. Slipping wheels (`get_skidinfo` vs `skid_grip_threshold`, now an export on the component) lay fading `SkidMark` ribbons tinted by the ground's `skid_color` (rubber-black on tarmac; the DESERT lays none at all — `sand.tres` sets `skid_marks = false`, checked in `drop_skid_mark` right after the surface lookup, so a ribbon that crosses onto sand simply stops getting points and ends itself on the idle timeout; `SkidMark` caches ONE material per colour, since a material per mark would be thousands) and drive the tire squeal (`tire_squeak.ogg` looping in the `AudioSqueal` node). A mark is ONE GROWING STRIP, not a trail of stamped quads: `Vehicle.drop_skid_mark` keeps a live `SkidMark` per wheel (`skid_trails`) and feeds it that wheel's contact point, and the mark rebuilds an `ImmediateMesh` TRIANGLE STRIP two vertices wide, offset sideways from the travel line (central difference, so the width stays square to the line THROUGH a corner rather than to the segment arriving at it) with UVs run by DISTANCE TRAVELLED. Quads could not do this — each one is straight, so a corner read as dashes; overlapping them to hide the seams stacked their alpha, which is why the old marks had to stay faint, and the surfaces' `skid_color` alpha was raised to make up the ~1.5 layers of overlap that are now gone. `POINT_SPACING` is a FLOOR on segment length, not the sampling rate — the wheel offers a point per physics frame and at speed that is already finer, so a value high enough to bite just quantises the strip to whole frames and leaves the head lagging the tyre. The tint is sampled UNDER THE WHEEL each time (`SurfaceData.at` at the contact point), NOT reused from the dust's `ground`: that one rays the chassis centre on a 0.25s timer, so it is over a metre sideways and up to a corner's worth of travel stale, which laid tarmac-black marks out on the sand and sand-brown ones down the middle of the road. A stroke is CHOPPED into chunks that butt together on a shared vertex — at `MAX_POINTS`, so the tail fades before the head (one node for a whole drift would blink out in one piece), and on any TINT CHANGE, which is what puts a hard colour edge exactly on the kerb. Only the two ends of the WHOLE stroke taper, by vertex alpha, and it is the HAND-OVER that says so (`handed_over`, set on the predecessor by `continue_from`) rather than the chunk being full — a chunk cut short at a kerb is mid-track and must butt, or the mark fades to nothing and restarts at the boundary. Keep chunks short enough that the fade step between neighbours stays under a tenth of the mark's alpha. A stroke ENDS ITSELF after `IDLE_TIMEOUT` without a new point, so nothing has to notice the wheel regripping (or the car being freed mid-slide). The streak texture (`make_skid_streak.gd`) is soft across the WIDTH and TILES down the length — the strip repeats it by distance, so anything that faded at v=0/1 (the old quad's end caps) would print a gap every repeat. The grip threshold barely gates any of this: measured over one hard turn, 0.70 vs 0.55 was 83 marks vs 80 because a real slide sits well under both, and 0.45 left NOTHING at all, rear dust emitters draw the soft `sand_puff.png` sprite (generated by `_tools/asset_extractor/make_sand_puff.gd` — the kit's only smoke art is a 10-frame muzzle sheet at 32px a frame whose LAST TWO FRAMES ARE HOLLOW RINGS, so with random anim offsets a fifth of the dust rendered as blurry donuts), scale with speed/slip and take their COLOUR AND VOLUME from the ground; the puffs also GROW AND LINGER with speed (`dust_pace` drives `scale_max` and `lifetime` between the `dust_speed_size`/`dust_speed_life` spans, authored look ≈ mid-speed) — RATE-LIMITED (`DUST_ADAPT_RATE`), because both properties are LIVE on airborne particles (scale re-reads its uniform per frame, a lifetime write rephases every fade) and a snap change deflates the plume hanging behind the car (`sample_ground`: one ray on a 0.25s timer, since the terrain reports a single tag map-wide) — the dust `ParticleProcessMaterial` is `resource_local_to_scene` precisely so recolouring is that car's alone, or every vehicle on the map would turn the colour of whichever one last crossed a kerb. There are TWO BANKS of rear emitters and `sample_ground` hands over between them on a surface change, because particle colour is a shader UNIFORM: writing it recolours every particle already in the air, so crossing a kerb repainted the entire plume hanging back down the road in one frame. The bank that was emitting simply stops and lives its particles out in the colour they were born with while the other takes over — banks are colour-stable, so alternating between two surfaces always swaps back to a bank whose leftovers already match. Tuning a plume: `dust_color` on the SURFACE is the authority (the value in vehicle_base.tscn is only the pre-`sample_ground` fallback, so editing it alone changes nothing); density is the alpha ramp; and `scale_min` matters more than `amount` — particles born smaller than their spawn spacing read as a string of separate beads until they have grown enough to overlap; and while driving the vehicle feeds `CameraFollow.look_ahead` with the travel direction. Braking while turning above `drift_min_speed` drifts: rear `wheel_friction_slip` drops to `drift_rear_grip`, the brake softens to `drift_brake_force`, and a speed-scaled `drift_yaw_assist` torque rotates the chassis; grip eases back on release. Damage states off `VehicleData.health_max`: smoke ≤50%, shader flame ≤30% — and THE FIRE'S STATE IS `Flame.burning`, NEVER `visible`. A flame is lit and put out through `fade_in`/`fade_out` ONLY, because `fade_out` is the one place the looping fire audio stops: the tier test used to hide the quad with `visible = false`, which killed the picture and left the loop roaring on a repaired car for the rest of the session. `visible` also LAGS the state by the second-long fade, so reading it back as the flag would refuse to re-light a car that dropped under the tier again mid-fade, leaving a burning wreck dark for good — `car_fire_probe` (vfx/) pins both halves: it damages a car under the tier, repairs it with a screwdriver, and asserts the loop stops and the car can still catch fire afterwards. Below 50% engine power also fades (1.0 → 0.4 at zero HP) and the engine loop swaps to `VehicleData.engine_malfunction_audio`; at 0 an OCCUPANT dies with the car (pulled out first so the corpse isn't freed with the wreck, killed, flung by the blast) and the car spawns the self-contained `scenes/vfx/explosion.tscn` (fireball rising into black smoke, fire spikes, flare, light, camera-kick; frees itself, and leaves a `ScorchMark` ground decal — random cell of `explosion_marks.jpg` with cooling ember glow), scatters every VISIBLE ArrayMesh piece as a charred `Debris` RigidBody (launched outward, sinks and frees after a few seconds; HIDDEN meshes — the car's stowed RoofRack — were never part of the silhouette and stay out of the wreckage), plus 4-5 BURNING COGS out of the engine bay (the Resource Bits `cog.tres` mesh): launched HIGH rather than far (`Debris.launch_spread` 0.8 / `launch_lift` 2.0 — the top-down camera loses anything that travels, so they fountain up and land on screen), EVERY piece — panels and cogs alike — leaves the blast FORGE-HOT and cools onto the char: the hurt flash's own trick (an unshaded `material_overlay` in orange over the shared char material), held `HEAT_HOLD` then fading over `HEAT_COOL`, both JITTERED per piece so the wreck dims unevenly instead of on one clock. `flaming` pieces additionally drag a world-space additive fire trail (`local_coords` off so the fire hangs where the flight left it, `fixed_fps` 0 or the trail beads — the sand-trail lesson; `preprocess` 0.12 so the fire exists AT launch instead of a metre up the arc), whose open flame dies on its own `BURN_TIME` clock around touchdown — guttering, since airborne puffs live out their half second — while the glow lingers on the metal, and they smolder `FLAME_EXTRA_BURN` longer before sinking. `wreck_probe` (vfx/) pins the exclusion, the single cog, its speed edge and the trail; `wreck_shot` is the windowed look check. Then the car `queue_free`s.
