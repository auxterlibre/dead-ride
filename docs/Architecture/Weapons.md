# Weapons

## Weapons (`scripts/weapons/`)

The shot line is `body centre → PlayerAim.aim_position`, flat at the aim plane (the equipped weapon's live muzzle height), so with deviation zeroed a shot lands EXACTLY under the reticle — `aim_yaw_offset` only swings the chest look-at that points the visible barrel and can never move a bullet. `PlayerAim` picks a target column under the cursor so a head or the car roof is aimed THROUGH, but it must ignore GROUND hits (`is_ground_hit`: floor-ish normal, below the plane, never a HurtBox): the terrain shares the walls layer, so the pick lands on it constantly, and raising that spot to fire height threw the aim point metres past the reticle — ~60px of screen error per metre of hold height, so a pistol held at 1.41m read half again as wrong as a rifle at 1.06m. Surfaces ABOVE the plane are still picked; that is the car-roof case.

`Weapon` → `WeaponMelee` / `WeaponRanged`. Ranged weapons are HITSCAN: rays from the aim plane toward the reticle with damage falloff past `WeaponData.effective_range`, per-pellet spread, and travel-time-delayed hit feedback. `SurfaceData` (`data/surfaces/<tag>.tres`, matched to a body's `surface` metadata) is the one ground-material table: bullet holes, impact audio/burst, footstep audio, ricochet, AND vehicle dust (`dust_color` + `dust_amount`, 0 = throws nothing) and skid marks (`skid_color` + `skid_marks`, false = the surface records wheels some other way; sand is off, since displaced tracks and a ribbon over them are two marks for one slide). `SurfaceData.of(tag)` / `SurfaceData.under(node, mask)` / `SurfaceData.at(world, position, mask)` are the shared lookups — three call sites had grown their own copy of the path-build. `under` is `at` with the node's own position; `at` exists for callers with nothing standing on the spot they're asking about (a tyre's contact patch is metres from the node owning the wheel). The world is DESERT: the terrain maps tag `"sand"` (there is no `grass.tres`; it was renamed, having always played `step_sand.ogg`), and the roads tag `"cement"`, which is what stops a car raising a full sand plume on tarmac.

THE HAND IS NOT A LEGAL SPAWN POINT. `throw_origin()` is the hand bone CLAMPED — `clear_reach` sweeps the grenade's own sphere out from the body axis at the hand's height and stops at the last spot it can occupy — because the arm swings THROUGH whatever the player stands against. Beside the station's gas pump the release frame put the hand 0.5m inside the pump's collider, and a RigidBody born inside a static body is not thrown at all: Jolt ejects it at a speed of its own choosing (11.6 m/s became 8.2 with a 1.5 m/s sideways kick in ONE frame), differently every run from a launch that was otherwise bit-identical, so the landing scattered over 5m of forecourt. While jammed it is EXTRUDED, not integrated — position moving 0.1m per frame against a velocity of zero — and roughly one throw in five was extruded downward past the top of the 120x2x120 ground box, at which point the box's nearest face is its UNDERSIDE and the grenade is pushed out the bottom to fall forever. That last one reads exactly like tunnelling and is not: `continuous_cd` was already on, and 11 m/s covers 0.19m a tick against a 0.22m radius. `BODY_RADIUS` lives on PlayerThrow and ThrowPreview references it, the same arrangement as `WORLD_MASK`, so the swept trace and the clamp can never disagree about how big the grenade is. Residual: the preview traces from the WIND-UP hand and the release fires from the MARKER hand, about a metre apart, so the two still sweep different corridors — the landing is preserved (both re-solve to `locked_target`) but the obstacles each meets are not.

THE THROW'S READ-OUT IS ITS OWN COMPONENT — `ThrowPreview` (`scripts/components/player/throw_preview.gd`, the script ON player.tscn's TrajectoryPreview MeshInstance3D): the swept-ball arc trace with its bounce fitting, the pop easing, and the blast/core zone drawing all live there, pulling `launch`/`flight`/`revealed` from its `throw` export; `landing` is preview state, not throw state. PlayerThrow keeps aiming, solving, the swing lifecycle and release — the release re-solves from `locked_target` and never reads the trace. `WORLD_MASK` stays a PlayerThrow const the preview references, so the pick and the trace cannot drift apart. The explosives probes reach the trace/pop helpers through `throwing.preview` now.

VFX in `scripts/vfx/` (tracer streak, `BulletImpact` driven by `SurfaceData` files in `data/surfaces/` matched to bodies' `surface` metadata — a surface with `ricochet = true` skips the hole decal and instead flashes + launches a fresh `Tracer` off the mirror of the shot line (impacts are arrival-delayed, so it reads as the same bullet glancing off), `FleshImpact` blood for HurtBox hits — the spray, then half a second later 1-5 `BloodPool.drop()`s where the droplets land: damage nudges the count (`DROPLET_DAMAGE`, one extra per 5 real damage — pistols splash 2, the sniper 5), the first at the landing and the rest flung onward along the exit direction, scattered wide enough that outliers escape the coalescence and read as flecks). Weapon scenes in `scenes/weapons/` carry muzzle flash/smoke/audio nodes.

BLOOD POOLS (`shaders/blood_pool.gdshader`, `scenes/vfx/blood_pool.tscn`, `scripts/vfx/blood_pool.gd` — adapted from dip's "Bloody Pool!", godotshaders.com, MIT) replace the old splatter-quad decal: drops are shader uniform arrays (`positions`/`scales`, tween-driven grow → hold → dry-away) on a transparent overlay plane, and every pixel MULTIPLIES the soft radial drop textures then thresholds the product (`merge_factor`), which is what makes touching drops COALESCE into one blob and a moving bleeder leave a connected trail instead of stacked stamps. Pools carry a KIND now: `BloodPool.drop(parent, position, kind)` — "blood" (default; every old call site unchanged) or "fuel", the barrels' black stains — with one scene and ONE REGISTRY PER KIND (`fuel_pool.tscn` overrides only the material colorway and slower dry exports; `registry_for()` keeps blood's `tiles` keyed by plain Vector3i, which `blood_probe` reaches into directly). The planes live on a FIXED 6m WORLD GRID (keyed tile-x / rounded-y / tile-z — the y keeps a terrace's blood off the ground below): overlapping planes are forbidden because two alpha layers stack into a dark sliver wherever both draw, so tiles abut exactly and a drop whose texture skirt crosses a border is fed to BOTH tiles with the SAME rolled size — each side then computes an identical product field at the seam line and the blob continues across it pixel-exact. Off-plane drop centres sample fine because the drop texture (radial GradientTexture2D, black core to WHITE margin, authored in the .tscn) clamps white under `repeat_disable` — white is the multiply-identity, so out-of-reach texels are no-ops. The material sits in `material_override` with `resource_local_to_scene` — NOT inside the shared PlaneMesh, where local-to-scene would not reach through and every tile would share one drop array. `MAX_DROPS` (32) must match the shader const; a flooded tile skips new drops; an emptied tile erases its registry key BEFORE `queue_free` so a same-frame drop re-founds instead of feeding the corpse. Sizing: a drop's visible radius is where the gradient crosses `merge_factor`, roughly half its texture — drops tuned smaller than ~0.17 of the tile read as beads, not pools.

## Kit weapon scenes (2026-09-22)

The scenes under `scenes/items/weapons/kit/` are generated by `_tools/asset_extractor/build_weapons.tscn` from the Apocalypse_Weapons assemblies and the Zombies-kit tools; the six ranged `WeaponData` resources and the new `bat`/`crowbar` ones point at them. A kit gun is a modular assembly: a `Base` mesh with `Bolt`, `Trigger`, magazines, buttstocks, grips and barrels as children, plus `Place_*` socket markers (`_Muzzle`, `_Sight`, `_Magazine`, `_Buttstock`, `_Bar*`, `_Charm`). Every attachment the gun can take ships in the assembly, and the alternates sit off their sockets, which is what the zoo showed as spare parts strewn under each gun. The builder keeps the core parts and the first part sitting on each socket and hides the rest, so a variant is a visibility flag away.

Muzzle placement: the `_Muzzle` socket, pushed forward to the front of the visible geometry, because the SMG and shotgun barrels extend past their socket. The revolver placeholder has no muzzle socket, so it takes the front of its bounds just under the top. Kit guns are authored along +Z with the origin at the grip; the scene turns them onto the +X barrel axis that `EnemyWeapons.barrel_error` and the base scene's `ProjectileSpawn` assume.

Two traps met while building it. A kit assembly instantiated into the weapon and packed with its children owned saved every part TWICE: the assembly node kept its `scene_file_path`, so on load it re-instantiated its own children next to the hidden copies; clearing the path flattens it. And the lineup shot of the base showed no characters at all until the stage got a physical floor or locomotion was switched off, since `CharacterMovement` drops the body through a visual-only plane in a few frames.

Melee: `bat` (two-handed) and `crowbar` (one-handed) carry a HitBox over the top 60% of the tool. They are carried and posed but do not swing yet, because `CharacterWeapons.UPPER_ANIMATIONS` lists attack clips only for fists and the blend tree has no inputs for the melee attack clips the library already holds.
